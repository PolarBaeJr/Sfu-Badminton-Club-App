# 1 · What the App Is (and Why)

## The problem it solves

Running a badminton club means juggling a lot at once: who's a paid member this term, who's showing up to sessions, who's playing whom, who's actually any good, running tournaments without spreadsheets falling apart, and keeping the exec team coordinated. Most of that traditionally lives in a mess of group chats, Google Sheets, and one person's memory.

This app puts all of it in **one place**, with a competitive ladder at the center to keep members coming back.

## Who it's for

The app serves three groups, each with their own view:

### 🏸 Players (members)
The everyday experience. A player can:
- See their **live ranking** on the club ladder (separate ratings for singles and doubles).
- **Challenge** other members, play the match, and report the result — their rank updates automatically.
- Browse and **check in to sessions** (competitive or recreational).
- **Register for tournaments** and check in on event day.
- Track their own stats — win/loss record, streaks, rating history.

### 🎓 Executives
The exec team gets access to a private **admin console** to run the club:
- Manage **sessions**, **tournaments**, **matches**, and **announcements**.
- See who's attending and participating.
- (Financial and member-management tools are restricted to admins — see below.)

### 🔑 Admins
Full control. On top of everything execs can do, admins handle the sensitive parts: **fees**, **member records**, **disputes**, and **club settings**. Access is deliberately tiered so that money and personal data are only touched by the people who should touch them.

## The three surfaces

The app is really three connected experiences:

| Surface | Who sees it | What it's for |
|---------|-------------|---------------|
| **Player app** | Logged-in members | The ladder, challenges, sessions, tournaments, personal stats |
| **Admin console** | Execs & admins | Running the club — behind a login and role checks |
| **Public site** | Anyone (no login) | A landing page with the live top of the ladder, the full public leaderboard, and the exec team roster |

The public site is a front door — it shows the club is active and competitive, which helps with recruitment, without exposing anything private.

## Why a ladder?

The **ELO rating system** (the same idea used in chess and competitive video games) is the engagement engine. Every ranked match moves your number. It gives casual members a reason to keep playing, gives strong players something to defend, and gives the club an objective, always-current picture of everyone's skill — which also powers fair tournament seeding.
